## 2. High-Engagement Content Patterns (Week of July 6–14)

### Pattern 1: "Timestamped Resource List" (winner)

**Best example:** @0xCodez Google agentic engineering course (546K views, 4K likes)
**Format:** `00:00 – Topic 1 / 08:24 – Topic 2 / 28:34 – Topic 3...`
**Why it works:** Saves time, looks like a TOC, drives completion rate. Universal across niches.
**Templates:**

- Video timestamps (Google course format)
- Chapter list for a long thread
- "Sections" header for a long-form article

### Pattern 2: "Call Out The Saturated Playbook, Then Pivot" (rising)

**Best example:** @LLAka662040's indie hacker call-out (6.8K views)
**Format:** `"Everyone does X, Y, Z. But here's what's actually working..."`
**Why it works:** Names the elephant → positions you as above the noise. Strong for BIP and AI wrapper content.

### Pattern 3: "First-of-Kind Demo" (nuclear)

**Best example:** Dilum Sanjaya's vibe-coded hardware prototype (110K views, 1,544 likes)
**Format:** `"Haven't seen any demos of [X] like this. So I made one."`
**Why it works:** Novelty × execution = the algorithm's favorite combo. Even small projects hit 6 figures if it's the first time someone has done it on the platform.

### Pattern 4: "Real Numbers + Real Playbook" (BIP workhorse)

**Best example:** Max Blade CNVS / $23,558 in 14 days (32K views, 961 bookmarks)
**Format:** `"I [past failure]. My last [thing] did [specific number] in [short timeframe]. Here's the EXACT playbook: 01:33 [chapter] / 02:38 [chapter]..."`
**Why it works:** Bookmarks = private saves = compounding authority. Timestamp format makes the playbook feel "complete" and shareable.

### Pattern 5: "Specific Tools &gt; Generic AI Talk" (carried over from March, still king)

**Best example:** @hiimeprem's "5 tools" designer post (vs. @tomreia's 120-tool thread that underperformed in saves)
**Format:** `5 tools → outcome. Not 25 tools → overwhelm.`
**Why it works:** Audience wants copy-paste stacks, not catalogs. The 120-tool list went wide but didn't save.

### Pattern 6: "Anti-Hype / Curated-Negative" (rising)

**Best example:** @hiimeprem "the ones actually worth using (not the hyped ones)"
**Format:** `"Most [X] are [mediocre take]. Here's the [small N] that earned a permanent spot."`
**Why it works:** The "no" gives you credibility the "yes" never can. Works for tools, books, podcasts, anything listicle-shaped.

### What Doesn't Perform (this week)

- Generic "100 AI tools for 2026" carousels — over-saturated, low saves
- Abstract AI speculation without tool names or specific outcomes
- "AI will replace X" doom takes without an angle
- Threaded essays without a scannable structure (no timestamps/chapters)
- Long-form quote-tweets without a fresh take

### Avoid

- **Monday morning** (inbox overload, low save rate)
- **Late night (11 PM+ ET)** unless targeting APAC
- **Sunday** for any work-themed posts

### Frequency

- **3–5 posts/week** is the sweet spot. Consistency &gt; volume.
- **1 long thread/week max.** Wednesday or Thursday is the strongest thread day.
- **Mix formats:** 3 short posts + 1 thread + 1 reply-bait engagement tweet.

## 5. High-Potential Underserved Topics (Fresh Gaps)

### Gap 1: "Anti-AI Aesthetic" / Artisanal Design

**Why underserved:** AI slop is everywhere. The first wave of "intentionally human" design content is forming (per @designertom's call-out) but no one has built a coherent thread yet.
**Angle:** "The most viral designs of late 2026 all share one thing: visible human imperfection. Here's how to use it without looking like a gimmick."
**Tags:** `#Design` `#ArtisanalHuman` `#AntiAI`

### Gap 2: AI Agents for Physical / Real-World Tasks

**Why underserved:** Everyone covers AI agents for software. Dilum Sanjaya's hardware prototype hit 110K views specifically because *no one* has done it. The gap is the physical layer.
**Angle:** "I wired Claude Code to a \[Raspberry Pi / 3D printer / CNC / smart home\]. Here's what broke and what worked."
**Tags:** `#VibeCoding` `#AIagents` `#Hardware`

### Gap 3: Designer + AI Workflow (Not "AI for Designers")

**Why underserved:** Prèm's 5-tool designer thread is one of the few that hit. Most AI design content is for non-designers. Designers using AI as a *craft* tool is a thin niche.
**Angle:** "I let Claude handle my client emails, but I still hand-pick every kerning pair. Here's my 2026 designer stack."
**Tags:** `#Design` `#AItools` `#IndieDesigners`

### Gap 4: "First-to-Comment" Engagement Loops

**Why underserved:** Most "comment X for the guide" CTAs use a single word. The fromzerotomill thread uses a *compound* trigger ("comment 'VIRAL' + RT"). Few threads use this and it drives 3-5x more replies.
**Angle:** Use compound CTAs: "comment TOOL + follow", "comment GUIDE + RT", "DM me \[X\]".
**Tags:** `#BuildInPublic` `#LeadMagnet`

### Gap 5: "Vibe Coding Failure Modes"

**Why underserved:** Vibe coding success stories are everywhere. Failure postmortems are rare. Builders are desperate to know what breaks before they ship.
**Angle:** "I vibe-coded a \[thing\] and shipped in 2 days. Here's the bug that cost me a week — and the 3 things I'll do differently."
**Tags:** `#VibeCoding` `#BuildInPublic` `#Postmortem`
